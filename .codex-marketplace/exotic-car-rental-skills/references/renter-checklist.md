# Renter checklist: what to confirm before paying a deposit

Contents

- Red flags
- Insurance, plainly
- Age and license

Every skill that ends in "contact the company" hands the user this list, trimmed
to what the catalog did not already answer. Mark each item **published** (the
catalog shows it, with the link) or **ask** (not published).

## The eight questions

1. **All-in price.** Daily rate, taxes, delivery, cleaning, any "service" or
   "processing" fee. Ask for the total for your exact dates in writing.
2. **Security deposit.** Amount, card hold or charge, and how many days after
   return it is released.
3. **Mileage.** Miles included per day and the price per extra mile. A 300-mile
   weekend on a 75-mile-a-day car adds up fast.
4. **Insurance.** What you must carry, whether they accept your policy, and what
   they sell. See below.
5. **Age and license.** Minimum age, young-driver fee, years licensed,
   international license rules.
6. **Damage process.** Who inspects, with photos at pickup, and how damage is
   charged (repair estimate, loss-of-use per day, admin fee).
7. **Cancellation.** Refund rules and the deadline. Event weekends (Art Basel,
   F1, prom) are often non-refundable.
8. **Delivery and return.** Address, time window, late fee per hour.

## Red flags

Raise any of these plainly with the user:

- Payment only by wire, Zelle, crypto or gift card, or a deposit to a personal
  account
- No written rental agreement before payment
- A price far below the market range for the same model in that city (check
  `price_overview`): it is often an hourly rate, a bait listing or a scam
- No physical address, no company name on the contract, or a different name on
  the payment than on the listing
- Refusal to state the deposit or the insurance requirement in writing
- Pressure to pay "today to hold the car"

## Insurance, plainly

- Most personal auto policies cover a rental only up to the policy's limits and
  only for similar vehicles; many exclude exotic and high-value cars. The user
  should call their insurer and ask about a car of this value.
- Most credit card rental benefits exclude exotic, luxury and high-value
  vehicles. Do not tell the user their card covers it.
- Companies often require proof of full coverage before pickup, or sell a damage
  waiver. Ask what the waiver excludes and its deductible.
- This is general information, not insurance advice.

## Age and license

Minimum age for exotic rentals is commonly 21 or 25 and differs by company and
by car; `search_companies` with `max_min_age` filters by the published minimum.
Some companies charge a young-driver fee or ask for a larger deposit.

Only the renter and the drivers named on the agreement may drive, and each must
meet the minimum age. A friend or a teenager behind the wheel of a car rented
by someone else usually voids the agreement and the insurance.

## At pickup and return

- Walk around the car with the staff, photograph every panel, each wheel close
  up, the glass and the dashboard (mileage and fuel or charge), timestamped.
- Get a copy of the condition report.
- Return with the agreed fuel or charge and photograph the car again.
